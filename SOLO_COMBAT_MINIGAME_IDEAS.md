# Custom solo combat minigame ideas

Saved 2026-09-09 at the owner's request for later consideration. The owner liked The Crucible and The Bounty Board and asked to save all three ideas after hearing The Last Outpost. No selection or implementation was authorized. These are proposed designs; numbers, rewards and mechanics remain open to tuning.

## 1. The Crucible

A short solo combat run where players assemble powerful combinations of temporary upgrades and decide whether to bank their winnings or risk another fight.

- Enter with personal equipment; death is safe for equipment but loses unbanked run rewards.
- Six rooms and a final boss, targeting 10–15 minutes.
- Choose one of three random combat upgrades after each room, with useful options for the player's equipment.
- Two cash-out checkpoints; continuing offers better rewards.
- Completion unlocks harder tiers with additional enemy mechanics.

Example upgrades, active only inside the minigame:

| Upgrade | Proposed effect |
| --- | --- |
| Chain Reaction | Enemy deaths damage nearby enemies. |
| Blood Price | Increased damage with reduced healing effectiveness. |
| Last Stand | Increased damage at low life points. |
| Arcane Echo | Every fourth successful damaging spell produces a weaker second hit. |
| Executioner | Defeating a strong enemy restores some special attack energy. |
| Glass Cannon | Increased damage dealt and received. |

Example encounters:

- The Ritual: interrupt cultists before they empower a champion.
- Marked for Death: defeat a marked enemy protected by allies.
- The Hunt: pursue a dangerous ranger while avoiding clearly signalled attacks.
- The Twins: manage two enemies that become stronger if one dies too early.
- The Warden: final boss using movement, prayer and target switching.

Rewards: Crucible tokens, a modest completion chest, cosmetic milestones, supplies, titles and a distinctive cape or outfit. A carefully balanced signature item could come later. Progression primarily unlocks choices and challenges rather than continually increasing item stats.

First-version scope: one arena theme, six room encounters, twelve upgrades, one boss and three difficulty levels, using existing NPCs and objects where practical.

Main appeal: short runs, upgrade synergies and optional cash-out decisions encourage replay.

## 2. The Bounty Board

Solo custom monster hunts in private arenas, focused on one memorable encounter lasting about 5–8 minutes. This is a PvM concept, separate from the older player-bounty proposal in ideas.txt.

- Choose between three contracts showing the monster, special traits and reward.
- Visible contract modifiers add variation, such as exploding minions, moving hazards or an enraged final phase.
- Prepare for the chosen challenge, then enter its private arena.
- After winning, bank the reward or carry it into a harder hunt for a multiplier, chaining up to three contracts.
- Death loses the unbanked bounty; personal equipment remains safe.

Example targets:

- The Blood Knight: heals through attacks; destroy blood fountains to cut off healing.
- The Hollow Archer: moves between cover and marks tiles for arrow volleys.
- The Gravekeeper: summons servants carrying pieces of his protective shield; defeat them to expose him.
- The Mimic: copies the player's combat style; timely switches exploit its next form.

Rewards: hunter ranks, cosmetic trophies, a bounty outfit and supply tokens. Optional objectives, such as interrupting every fountain or avoiding all arrow volleys, award bonus tokens without making speed the only measure of skill.

First-version scope: three well-made enemies, with new contracts added individually over time.

Main appeal: focused custom boss fights, preparation and optional consecutive-hunt risk.

## 3. The Last Outpost

A solo fortress-defence combat minigame. Players actively fight while deciding where to defend, what to repair and when to venture outside for extra resources. Target duration: 10–15 minutes.

Run flow:

1. Prepare: spend starting supplies on barricades, a defensive ballista or a healing well.
2. Defend: enemies attack different entrances, with scouts warning of incoming assaults.
3. Recover: collect supplies from defeated enemies and use short breaks for repairs or upgrades.
4. Venture out: optional enemy supply caravans offer extra upgrades but leave the fortress exposed while pursued.
5. Survive the siege: defeat a final boss attacking alongside its remaining army.

Enemy roles: brutes smash gates, sappers rush structures, necromancers revive attackers and archers pressure from outside the walls. Clear warnings make threats readable.

Limited supplies create decisions: fortify one entrance and personally defend another, buy crowd-control firepower or repair the central crystal, and decide whether a damaged gate can survive a caravan chase.

Equipment remains safe on defeat. Rewards depend on assaults survived, optional objectives and difficulty, with a completion bonus. Tokens could unlock fortress appearances, banners, cosmetics and alternative defensive structures with different strengths.

First-version scope: one compact fortress, two entrances, three build locations, five assaults and one siege boss. Use existing objects and NPC models to establish the gameplay first.

Main appeal: tactical defence and resource decisions alongside active combat.

## Resuming later

The Crucible emphasizes build variety; The Bounty Board emphasizes focused boss fights; The Last Outpost emphasizes tactical defence. Choose a concept with the owner before implementation. Existing managed instances are a potential foundation, but implementation must consult the current instance and combat reports and preserve the project's existing customizations and Summoning/Dungeoneering deferrals.
